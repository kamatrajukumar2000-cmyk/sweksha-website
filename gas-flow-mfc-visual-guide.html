CVD / MPCVD Gas Delivery & MFC Visual Guide

A mass flow controller (MFC) is one part of a larger gas-delivery chain. This animated illustration shows the general relationship between a gas source, pressure regulation, isolation, flow control and a process chamber.

Important: This is a conceptual learning diagram, not a piping schematic. Real layouts, component order, gas compatibility, pressure limits and interlocks depend on the machine manufacturer and installed configuration.

What each component does

Gas source and supply

Provides the specified process gas. Correct gas identity, cylinder handling and approved supply arrangements are essential.

Pressure regulator

Reduces and controls upstream supply pressure within the component's rated specification. It is not a substitute for an MFC.

Isolation valve

Allows an approved system section to be isolated. Valve position and interlocks must follow the machine's operating procedure.

Mass flow controller

Measures and regulates gas flow according to its calibration, gas configuration, range and operating conditions.

Controller / PLC / HMI

Displays commands, readings, alarms and communication status. Capture the exact message before diagnosis.

Chamber connection

Delivers gas to the configured process area through model-specific tubing, fittings and distribution hardware.

When the MFC reading looks wrong

  • Record the MFC manufacturer, model, serial number and calibrated gas shown on its label.
  • Record commanded setpoint, displayed actual flow, units, alarm text and timestamp.
  • Record available upstream pressure indication and whether the reading is steady or fluctuating.
  • Note recent maintenance, gas-cylinder change, configuration change or communication interruption.
  • Have qualified personnel compare the installation with the manufacturer's approved specifications.

Safety: Do not loosen gas fittings, bypass interlocks, change a process recipe, adjust regulator settings or attempt calibration based on this guide. Gas systems can contain hazardous or pressurized gases. Use approved isolation, purge, leak-check and service procedures with trained personnel.
