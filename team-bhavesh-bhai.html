07 / IMPORT ROADMAPForeign Purchase: Enquiry to DeliveryComplete step-by-step overseas procurement journey

Each international purchase is handled as a documented project. SWEKSHA coordinates the buyer, overseas supplier and relevant logistics or clearance professionals; the buyer approves the order and all financial commitments.

  • Step 1 — Requirement: confirm product, use, model, quantity, new/refurbished condition, destination and target delivery.
  • Step 2 — Supplier search: identify possible manufacturers or authorised distributors and request company and product details.
  • Step 3 — Technical comparison: compare specification, compatibility, accessories, warranty, manuals and after-sales support.
  • Step 4 — Commercial offer: compare currency, price, minimum order, lead time, payment terms and delivery basis.
  • Step 5 — Verification: check supplier identity, references, product evidence and independent inspection options before payment.
  • Step 6 — Import readiness: confirm Indian import eligibility, applicable policy, licences and product approvals with authorised specialists.
  • Step 7 — Purchase: approve the proforma invoice, contract, payment milestones and shipping terms.
  • Step 8 — Dispatch: coordinate packing, insurance, freight booking and shipment documents.
  • Step 9 — Clearance: importer and appointed customs broker handle filing, assessment, duties and release.
  • Step 10 — Delivery: inspect received goods, record shortages or damage and follow up on warranty and installation.
08 / IMPORT DOCUMENTSImport Documents & India ReadinessWhat is usually checked before goods leave the supplier

Exact documents depend on product, origin, country of export, Indian import policy and transaction terms. A document checklist is prepared for the specific shipment, not assumed to be identical for every product.

  • Importer details, IEC and GST information where applicable
  • Supplier quotation, purchase order and proforma/commercial invoice
  • Packing list with quantity, weight, dimensions and package marks
  • Air Waybill or Bill of Lading and freight/insurance records
  • Country-of-origin evidence or certificate where required
  • Technical catalogue, model/serial details and product declarations
  • Applicable licences, NOCs, registrations or product approvals
  • Customs broker instructions and supporting classification information

HS classification, valuation, duty, restrictions and approvals must be confirmed for the exact goods by the importer and authorised customs/trade professionals before dispatch.

09 / HS CODE & TRADE TERMSHS Classification, Incoterms & ResponsibilitiesUnderstand who pays, arranges and bears each shipment risk

Commercial comparisons are meaningful only when the delivery basis and responsibilities are understood. Incoterms® rules allocate specified delivery obligations, costs and risk between seller and buyer; they do not replace the sales contract or determine every legal issue.

  • Collect the product's technical description, material, function and intended use for HS-code review.
  • Compare supplier quotations on a consistent delivery basis such as EXW, FCA, FOB, CPT, CIP, DAP or DDP where suitable.
  • Identify who arranges export clearance, main carriage, insurance and import clearance.
  • Confirm the named place/port and the exact Incoterms® rule and version in the contract.
  • Ask the customs broker to verify classification, valuation and applicable duty before order approval.

Never select a tariff code or delivery term solely from a supplier's informal message; obtain professional confirmation.

10 / PAYMENT & FOREIGN EXCHANGEInternational Payments & Banking CoordinationPayment milestones, bank documents and fraud checks

Cross-border payments should follow the buyer's bank requirements, applicable foreign-exchange rules and the agreed purchase contract. SWEKSHA can organise information and coordinate questions but does not act as a bank or authorise remittances.

  • Match beneficiary name and bank details against independently verified supplier records.
  • Check invoice currency, amount, payment milestones, bank charges and due dates.
  • Discuss advance payment, documentary collection or letter-of-credit options with the buyer's authorised bank where relevant.
  • Confirm required import remittance purpose codes, documents and reporting with the bank/authorised dealer.
  • Verify any last-minute bank-account change through a known, independently verified contact channel.
  • Retain payment advice, bank confirmations and supplier acknowledgements.

Do not send funds based only on an email requesting changed bank details. The importer and bank must confirm compliance.

11 / FREIGHT & INSURANCEAir Cargo, Sea Freight & Shipment TrackingChoose the transport plan for the shipment

Transport selection depends on shipment size, urgency, fragility, value, hazardous classification, destination and total landed cost.

  • Compare air freight for urgent/high-value shipments and sea freight for suitable larger or less time-sensitive cargo.
  • Collect package dimensions, gross/net weight, packing type, pickup address and destination.
  • Confirm cargo acceptance, restricted-goods rules and special handling with the carrier.
  • Review transit time, routing, trans-shipment, local handling and destination charges.
  • Coordinate cargo insurance scope, insured value, exclusions and claim process.
  • Track AWB/BL milestones, arrival notice, delivery order and final delivery.
12 / CUSTOMS & LANDED COSTIndian Customs Clearance & Total Landed CostEstimate the complete cost before purchase approval

The landed-cost sheet helps the buyer see the likely total cost rather than comparing only the supplier's unit price. Actual assessment is determined by customs and applicable authorities.

  • Goods value and agreed Incoterms basis
  • Origin transport, export handling and freight
  • Insurance and other includible valuation elements as applicable
  • HS-code-based basic customs duty and other applicable levies
  • Port/airport handling, customs broker and documentation charges
  • Local transport, unloading, installation and commissioning estimates
  • Bank conversion rate, transfer charges and contingency assumptions

All figures are labelled as estimates until confirmed by the carrier, bank, customs broker and relevant authorities.

13 / QUALITY & RISKSupplier Due Diligence & Pre-Dispatch InspectionReduce avoidable commercial and technical risks

Before a buyer commits, available evidence should be checked against the product specification and purchase contract.

  • Verify supplier's legal identity, business address and authorised contact.
  • Request current product photographs, serial/model details and test records.
  • For machinery, review configuration, operating hours, software, utilities and included accessories.
  • Define inspection criteria, acceptance tests and independent inspector scope in writing.
  • Record packing condition, serial numbers and shipment photographs before dispatch.
  • Agree how defects, shortages, transit damage and warranty claims will be reported.

Verification reduces risk but cannot guarantee supplier performance or product condition.

14 / INDIA EXPORTSExport from India to Overseas BuyersSupport Indian suppliers with international sales coordination

Export work starts by checking the product, destination market and buyer requirements. Export eligibility and destination-country requirements vary by product and must be verified before accepting an order.

  • Capture buyer enquiry, specification, quantity and delivery destination.
  • Confirm Indian exporter details, IEC and product-specific export conditions as applicable.
  • Prepare quotation and agree currency, payment protection and delivery terms.
  • Coordinate packing list, commercial invoice, shipping bill and transport documents.
  • Check certificate of origin, inspection or destination-country documents if required.
  • Coordinate freight, insurance, customs broker and shipment tracking.
  • Follow up on proof of export, payment realisation and records with the exporter and bank.
15 / AFTER-SALESInstallation, Spares & Warranty CoordinationSupport after imported equipment reaches the customer

For machinery and technical equipment, purchase planning should include the support needed after delivery.

  • Confirm installation scope, site utilities and commissioning responsibilities before ordering.
  • List critical spares, consumables, recommended service kits and lead times.
  • Clarify warranty start date, coverage, exclusions and response channel.
  • Arrange manuals, drawings, training and remote support access where agreed.
  • Record delivery inspection, installation sign-off and outstanding defects.
  • Coordinate claims and replacement parts with supplier and customer.
