Diamond Seed Preparation

Scientific guide to diamond seed substrates, surface preparation, handling, mounting and pre-growth inspection.

1. What is a diamond seed?

A diamond seed is a pre-existing diamond substrate used as the crystallographic starting surface for homoepitaxial chemical vapour deposition. The deposited diamond layer follows the underlying crystal structure when the substrate surface and growth conditions are suitable. The seed is a prepared input to the growth process; it is not made by the reactor during that run.

2. Substrate selection and inspection

Selection normally considers crystal orientation, usable growth area, thickness, flatness, surface finish, dislocation content, inclusions, edge quality and the intended process. Incoming inspection should record the substrate identity and dimensions and check for chips, cracks, scratches, contamination and handling damage. Optical inspection is useful for visible defects; more detailed characterization may require suitable metrology or supplier documentation.

Inspection checklist

  • Confirm substrate identity, orientation and traceability.
  • Record length, width, thickness and any specified tolerances.
  • Inspect the growth face and edges under suitable lighting and magnification.
  • Reject or segregate cracked, chipped or contaminated pieces according to the quality procedure.
  • Keep each substrate protected from dust, oils and accidental contact.

3. Cleaning and surface preparation

Surface preparation aims to remove particles, organic residues and other contamination that can interfere with nucleation or epitaxial growth. The correct cleaning sequence depends on substrate material, surface treatment, contamination type and the approved laboratory procedure. Use only compatible, validated cleaning chemistry and equipment. Rinse and dry using a controlled process that avoids redeposition, water marks and particulate contamination.

Some production routes use additional surface conditioning or nucleation treatments. These are not interchangeable universal steps: they must be qualified for the seed type and desired growth mode. Record the procedure, materials, batch identity and operator so that results can be traced.

4. Handling, mounting and thermal contact

Handle the seed with clean, suitable tools and avoid touching the active growth face. Place it on a compatible holder with the correct orientation and stable mechanical support. Poor seating, particles under the substrate or uneven thermal contact can create local temperature differences, stress concentrations or unstable positioning during evacuation and heating. Verify holder cleanliness, alignment and retention before closing the chamber.

5. Relationship to CVD growth

In MPCVD, microwave energy sustains a plasma containing reactive hydrogen and carbon-bearing species. Surface reactions on the heated diamond seed can add carbon to the diamond lattice. Growth behaviour depends on reactor geometry, plasma distribution, substrate temperature, pressure, gas composition, flow control, surface condition and the validated recipe. A recipe developed for one reactor or seed geometry should not be assumed safe or suitable for another.

Pre-run records

Record the seed ID, holder ID, cleaning batch, visual inspection, mounting orientation, chamber condition and approved recipe revision. This information helps connect process history with the final crystal quality.

6. Common quality issues

Surface contamination

May contribute to poor nucleation, non-diamond carbon or localized defects.

Chips and edge damage

Can reduce usable area and create mechanical or stress-related problems.

Non-uniform mounting

Can disturb thermal contact and lead to temperature non-uniformity.

Incorrect traceability

Makes it difficult to compare growth results across substrate lots and recipes.

7. Practical quality-control record

Maintain a controlled record with substrate ID, source/grade, orientation, dimensions, inspection result, cleaning procedure revision, mounting position, holder condition, process run ID and post-growth observations. This record supports repeatability and root-cause analysis.

Safety: Cleaning chemicals, heated holders, vacuum equipment and plasma systems require trained personnel, approved procedures and suitable PPE. Never improvise chemical mixtures or bypass equipment safeguards.
